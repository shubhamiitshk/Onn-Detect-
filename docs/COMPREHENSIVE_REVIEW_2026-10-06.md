# OnnDetect: complete codebase review and improvement plan

Review date: **6 October 2026**. Checkout: `C:/Users/sumit/OneDrive/Desktop/Onn-Detect-/`. Reviewed the current working tree, including the user's existing uncommitted changes. Research recommendations below are proposals, not measured improvements in this app.

**The strongest next step is to make the training-to-Android pipeline reproducible and correct, then train a diverse image detector and speech detector, and select compact models using independent tests on real phones.** Downloading millions of examples before repairing labels, splits, preprocessing, exports, and failure handling would consume compute without establishing trustworthy accuracy.

The app has a useful starting structure: separate image/audio components, very small bundled quantized models, explicit DSP references, a Kotlin fusion implementation, local history, and a working Android build. Its present evidence does **not** substantiate detection of every AI platform. A practical target is broad, measured coverage of specified generator families and common transformations, with an inconclusive outcome when evidence is insufficient. Neither pixel statistics nor sound statistics can guarantee the origin of every future or heavily transformed file.

**What “all AI photos and audio” should mean in the product**

Define the target labels before training. Fully generated photos, AI inpainting/object replacement, face swaps, ordinary color correction, camera computational HDR/denoising, scanned documents, CGI, drawings and screenshots are different conditions. Likewise TTS, voice cloning/conversion, a short synthetic splice, replayed speech, AI music and synthetic environmental sound are different tasks. A camera photo that has been denoised is not equivalent to a fabricated scene; human speech passed through a neural codec is not automatically impersonation or fabricated words.

Keep origin/production labels, edit extent, content type and confidence of dataset ground truth. The UI can report “likely synthetic evidence” and identify an implicated modality or interval. A negative content detector result should say “no strong synthetic evidence found,” with coverage/quality limits, rather than certify camera origin or a live speaker.

Evaluate Stable Diffusion variants, FLUX, GANs and other available families, plus exact versions of ChatGPT/OpenAI and Google/Nano Banana generators and their export modes. A platform name is insufficient: versions, settings, editing and postprocessing matter. Maintain a fresh held-out collection as platforms change; update bundled model releases deliberately. Offline inference permits offline-distributed updates without requiring a network request for each scan.

CIFAKE is useful for a fast pipeline smoke test, but 32px examples are a poor principal training target for detailed present-day phone photos. Fake-only sources such as prompt-generation archives require a carefully matched real class; increasing only fake volume creates class/source imbalance.

Real examples deserve equal investment: varied phones/cameras, day/night/HDR/portrait modes, blur/noise, dark skin and diverse demographics, clean professional photography, social-media and messaging exports, screenshots, recaptured prints/screens, scans, illustrations/CGI, and normal editing. Include enough non-AI difficult negatives to measure false accusations. Establish origin from reliable source records and sampling audits; a web-crawl label or photography subreddit alone is not proof of a camera original.

For a usable speech pilot, start with official ASVspoof training data plus a controlled multilingual subset and matched real recordings. Add synthesis families/languages/channel conditions based on measured coverage gaps, rather than repeatedly resampling a small corpus. Real data should include phones, microphones, room acoustics, speech over background music, messaging/VoIP codecs, codec chains, clipped/quiet speech, pauses and short recordings.

For current commercial voice generators, acquire permitted, documented examples using matched transcripts and consented real speakers, and keep a separate fresh test collection. SpeechFake's unreleased API examples, old ASVspoof results and a trained model's internet demo do not demonstrate coverage of the latest OpenAI, ElevenLabs, Google or other voice platforms.

**“All audio” requires more than a speech anti-spoof model.** A speech classifier should not silently classify instrumental music, game audio or environmental recordings as human versus AI voice. Add routing/quality states for speech, music, environmental sound and mixed audio. Then evaluate distinct detector heads/data, with an unsupported/inconclusive result while a domain is unvalidated. Useful sources are [SONICS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.14080) / [release](https://huggingface.co/datasets/awsaf49/sonics) for human/AI music (about 97k songs / 4,751h, including Suno/Udio; noncommercial terms), [FakeMusicCaps](https://github.com/polimi-ispl/FakeMusicCaps) for generated music, and [FakeSound](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08052) / [project](https://FakeSoundData.github.io) for environmental/partially manipulated sound. Their generators, rights and evaluation tasks differ; none supplies a universal audio label on its own.

**Model choices: benchmark large teachers, deploy small validated students**

Training on a GPU workstation or server is compatible with **fully offline phone inference**. The distributed app should contain the runtime, model assets, frontend and calibration needed for first-run analysis without internet. No cloud classifier, API key, Play-services model download or remote verification should be a required step.

Choose a model using the same sealed data and operating point, then compare phone latency, memory, package footprint and degradation after quantization. Published accuracy/AP/EER figures on different datasets cannot rank candidates for this app. A smaller model that retains useful evidence after realistic transformations can be preferable to a high-scoring model evaluated only on one known dataset.

My first image deployment experiment would use one compact RGB/global branch at approximately 256px and one compact residual/local branch on native-resolution 224/256px crops. Train with the same whole-image/patch aggregation policy that the phone uses. Compare a single branch first, then add the other only if it improves held-out sensitivity at the selected false-positive rate enough to justify its cost. Start with a small deterministic patch set; assess additional coverage as an explicit deep-scan mode. Aspect-preserving sampling, original-resolution trace retention and calibrated image-level aggregation matter more than merely changing `IMG_SIZE`.

Supervised training on varied generators should remain the baseline. Add teacher soft targets only when the teacher improves held-out generalization and its licensing permits the intended derivative/use. Keep hard ground-truth labels, audit teacher errors on difficult real negatives, and hold the final evaluation set outside teacher fitting, student fitting and calibration. A large teacher can pass on source/encoding biases as well as useful signal.

For partial image editing, collect actual masks/region labels and mixed real/generated training examples before promising localization. A classifier saliency/Grad-CAM map can be an explanation visualization but is not a validated map of forged pixels.

Its training-corpus list includes ASVspoof 2021, ASVspoof 5 and SpoofCeleb: establish split/item non-overlap before calling those evaluations independent, including for a distilled student. |

My first speech model experiment would compare repaired LCNN, AASIST-L and RawTFNet variants on the **same** multilingual/source-balanced data and final Android runtime. Use a common validated decoding/resampling contract; feature choices may differ by model but must be reproduced on the phone. Keep the best compact model on measured recording-level sensitivity/false-positive/latency tradeoffs. Add teacher distillation or quantization-aware training only if it improves that result. Do not select a newly published “near-perfect” model solely from its paper's in-dataset score or implement it before verifying code, weights, protocol and export feasibility.

Treat voice activity detection as a usability/routing aid, not a guaranteed accuracy improvement. Compare retaining versus removing pauses and boundaries, because partial edits and synthesis clues can occur there. Speaker embeddings are an identity feature, not evidence of human origin: a voice clone can match the claimed identity. The present statistical placeholder in `SpeakerVerifier.kt` provides neither a trained speaker encoder nor anti-spoof verification.

**Data preparation and training that can support a reliable accuracy claim**

1. **Create one explicit dataset manifest.** Record dataset/revision, item ID, byte hash, decoded-media hash when practical, real/synthetic label, origin certainty, generator/model version, settings, prompt/transcript, parent photo/recording, speaker/session, language, edit mask/interval, real source/device, codec/resolution/duration and reuse terms. Use portable item IDs rather than OS-dependent path hashing. Count successfully decoded unique originals and source groups, not merely filenames. Quarantine corrupt media; do not replace it with black images or silence while retaining its label.

2. **Deduplicate and split by parent groups before augmentation or window extraction.** Exact hashes catch copied files; perceptual/audio fingerprints and metadata catch resized/recompressed copies and shared originals. Audit similarity thresholds so different legitimate examples are not merged indiscriminately. Keep original/edited/generated variants, prompt counterparts, utterance variants and overlapping windows together. Hold out speakers/sessions and generator families/versions when measuring transfer. Different LoRAs of one base generator are not necessarily independent families.

3. **Preserve published benchmark splits.** Use the training pool for optimizer fitting and internal out-of-fold stacking. Use selection-validation for architecture/epoch/hyperparameter decisions. Use a separate calibration pool for thresholds/probability fitting after quantization. Evaluate once on a sealed final test plus independent OOD suites. If continually consulting one test to choose changes, it has become development data; acquire a new final test. Track every teacher's pretraining/fine-tuning sources too: a distilled student can inherit benchmark exposure even when its own training manifest omits those files.

4. **Balance evidence rather than just file counts.** Sample across generator families, prompts, speakers, languages, real sources and channels. Limit the influence of thousands of near-identical generations from one original. Make genuine and synthetic classes share encoding/quality distributions and comparable subject matter. Validate against an unseen real source as well as an unseen generator. Check whether a metadata-only, resolution-only or JPEG-only baseline can predict labels; unusually strong shortcut baselines reveal dataset confounding.

5. **Use realistic, label-independent nuisance augmentation.** Images: varied JPEG/WebP compression, resize kernels/scales, crop, blur/noise, phone HDR/denoising, screenshots/recapture and plausible editing, applied to both classes with matched distributions. Preserve native evidence in some examples and test compounded transformations. A mandatory JPEG Q90 re-encode is not a universal cure; it may remove useful traces. Do not prescribe blanket rotation/color-normalization bans without ablations. Audio: validated sample-rate conversion, MP3/AAC/Opus/telephony chains, room/microphone response, noise, gain/clipping, replay and partial splices. Keep untransformed controls and inspect whether augmentations accidentally change the target label.

6. **Make the experiment configuration authoritative.** Load the chosen YAML/config and fail on unused/unknown fields. Record actual input size, frontend ID, model definition, label polarity, split fingerprints, seed, optimizer/scheduler, precision, effective batch size and sampling weights. Repair worker=0 handling, gradient-accumulation tails, checkpoint/resume state and advertised-but-absent EMA behavior before relying on them. Prefer numerically stable logit losses where appropriate, and check NaN/loss/gradient/label statistics on the pilot.

7. **Scale I/O before scale.** Use lazy reads, sharded compressed storage, bounded shuffle and bounded feature caches. The current eager image caching strategy is unsuitable for millions of high-resolution examples: one million raw 512x512 RGB images alone occupy about **786GB** before Python overhead. Keep augmentation on demand. Cache LFCC/teacher outputs only with media/frontend/model hashes and clear size budgets. Resume partial downloads with verification and stage them atomically; do not delete the previous valid corpus before a replacement succeeds.

8. **Run controlled learning curves and ablations.** Compare the repaired baseline, stronger single model, local+global combination, extra datasets, teacher distillation, augmentation and quantization as separate experiments. First keep architecture/training budget stable while testing dataset composition; then compare models on that chosen mixture. Repeat finalists with more than one seed when model-selection differences are small. Expand sample count when the learning curve and held-out coverage justify it, not simply because a larger archive exists.

**Evaluation: measure useful detection, false accusations and coverage**

The release report should include the exact final exported model and Android pipeline, not only a Python checkpoint. Report ROC-AUC and PR-AUC, TPR/recall at preselected FPR targets (for example 1% and 0.1%), balanced accuracy/confusion matrices, speech EER where relevant, and calibration reliability/Brier/ECE. Give sample/source-group counts and confidence intervals. Very small real test sets cannot substantiate a precise low-FPR claim. Report false positives per genuine **file/recording**, not only per patch or four-second window.

Break results down by generator family/version, real source, clean versus transformed content, image resolution/format/edit extent, language/speaker/accent, duration and audio codec/recapture condition. Assess demographic performance where the ground truth and sample sizes support it. Show the worst important slices, not just a macro average. Evaluate unknown inputs, poor-quality/too-short media and inference failures; publish abstention rate and performance at the resulting accepted coverage. An uncertainty policy itself must be tested, because unfamiliar inputs can still receive overconfident neural scores.

For partial images, evaluate region-level localization only when there are valid masks. For partial speech, measure edit-interval recall and false alarms in untouched intervals, including edits outside the center of a recording. For video, label and test four combinations: genuine visuals/genuine audio, synthetic visuals/genuine audio, genuine visuals/synthetic audio, both synthetic. Also cover missing audio, still-image slideshows, scene cuts, dubbing and natural visual edits. A frame-level still-image model plus a speech classifier is a screening system; it has not thereby become a validated temporal or lip-sync detector.

Do not average/max/OR additional windows without re-evaluating the aggregate: each extra opportunity can raise file-level false-positive rate. Learn a simple calibrated aggregation on development data using modality/quality/missingness, or use a clearly specified policy with its own tested operating point. Preserve separate modality evidence in the result. Start fusion with a simple logistic/calibrated late-fusion baseline; retain XGBoost or engineered FFT/KL features only if their matched mobile outputs add independent held-out benefit.

Raw classifier score is not automatically the probability that an arbitrary incoming file was generated by AI. Deployment prevalence changes interpretation: in an illustrative set of 10,000 files with 1% AI prevalence, 80% sensitivity and 1% FPR would give 80 true detections and 99 false detections, about **45% precision**. This is arithmetic, not an app benchmark, and explains why real-negative coverage and honest uncertainty deserve as much attention as fake-class accuracy.

Use a release acceptance sheet agreed before the final test. Suggested gates are: no status/failure-contract violations; no label/model/frontend mismatch; validated performance at the chosen FPR across target platform/language slices; bounded quantization degradation; truthful evaluated coverage; and acceptable measured phone performance. The exact sensitivity target must follow pilot results and product cost of false positives; inventing a promised 95/99% accuracy now would be misleading.

**Export and Android parity: one release transaction**

The current manual PyTorch-to-Keras reconstruction can work only when every operation, weight layout, padding, MFM behavior and frontend matches. Prototype [Google's LiteRT Torch converter](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/litert-torch) as an alternative to reduce duplicated architecture code. Its documented converter environment is **Linux**, so use a pinned Linux container/WSL/server conversion job rather than assuming native Windows compatibility. Operator/delegate coverage must be tested for each candidate; switching converters is not a guaranteed successful export.

Export should produce a staged bundle containing model(s), frontend/input contract, feature schema, Kotlin fusion or a small fusion artifact, histogram/constants, decision/calibration policy, supported media/modalities and a report. The manifest should link source revision, config/data/split fingerprints, checkpoint SHA-256, exported-model SHA-256, generated-code/constants hashes, runtime/operator versions and label orientation. Install that complete bundle into Android only after all gates succeed.

Check FP32 PyTorch versus the exported FP32 graph before quantization. Select a random stratified representative subset **from training**, including both classes, realistic channels/quality and difficult examples. Compare FP32 and INT8 score distributions, AUC, TPR at the release FPR, decisions at the real operating threshold and per-slice failures. Fit final calibration on final INT8 outputs from the calibration partition. Quantization-aware training is an option if post-training quantization harms the measured target; it should not be added by default without that evidence.

Use shared actual JPEG/PNG/WebP/HEIC/AVIF and WAV/FLAC/MP3/AAC/Opus fixtures as supported by the final platform. Compare raw file decoding, orientation/color/alpha, resampling, crops/windows, intermediate features and final model/fusion outputs across Python and Android. Add impulse/tone/silence fixtures and natural images/speech; the latter catch decoder and practical precision effects that mathematical reference arrays miss. Check representative low/near-threshold/high scores and missing values in the actual generated Kotlin tree, rather than testing only Python's reconstruction of its own AST.

Run instrumentation with the **bundled assets** on CPU and each supported delegate, using the exact release runtime. Validate shape/dtype/quantization parameters and finite outputs at startup/invocation. Preserve a deterministic CPU fallback only when it passes the same accuracy gates. Benchmark GPU/NPU/CPU end to end, including input copies, initialization and unsupported-op fallback; tiny models can be faster on CPU, but an unconditional CPU policy is not proof of optimal performance.

Make required validators fail if a dependency/model/report is missing, if one class is absent, or if tests are skipped. A successful exit from the present parity/export helper scripts sometimes means a check was not executed. Add fault cases for malformed/missing manifest, model failure, bad tensor output, damaged media, memory abstention, cancelled decode and concurrent scan requests. These are useful tests because they protect actual user-visible decisions, rather than merely duplicating implementation.

These are design targets, not observed results or guaranteed requirements. AASIST/RawTFNet operation cost, native runtime buffers and high-resolution decoding may dominate tiny parameter counts. Audio should also report processing time relative to assessed duration and thermal behavior for repeated/long scans. Reduce the budget or offer explicit deep scanning if the intended lower-end phones require it.

**Provenance and watermark support should be separate, verified evidence**

The current first-64KiB `c2pa.org` substring check cannot verify Content Credentials or distinguish camera capture from AI generation. For offline credential support, prototype a maintained verifier such as [c2pa-rs](https://github.com/contentauth/c2pa-rs) against the [C2PA specification](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.4/specs/C2PA_Specification.html): validate structure, signature, asset binding, signer/trust policy and actual generation/edit assertions. Package a versioned trust policy and make unavailable remote manifests or stale revocation information explicit. A valid signed credential is not automatically an authentic-camera claim; read what the signer asserts.

Missing credentials or watermark evidence is neutral. Ordinary unsigned EXIF, Photoshop/Lightroom tags, stripped metadata or square dimensions should not move an origin verdict by rule. A real photographer may have these characteristics, and an attacker can supply them. Evaluate any metadata model separately against source-balanced and stripped/injected-metadata tests.

[Google SynthID](https://deepmind.google/models/synthid/) supports provider-specific watermarking/detection products, but the current repo has no working image/audio watermark detector. Do not infer watermark presence from ASCII keywords or expose a trusted Boolean that forces score 1.0 without a genuine verified detector. Public detector access does not automatically provide a supported universal offline SDK or cover nonparticipating platforms. Positive validated generation assertions can add evidence; they do not remove the need for content detection where provenance is absent.

**A practical training plan for this machine**

Observed system memory is **7,887,036,416 bytes, about 7.35GiB** (marketed as 8GB). `nvidia-smi` confirms **RTX 3050 Laptop GPU, 4,096MiB VRAM**. The disk probe during review showed roughly **100GiB free on C:**; this changes as builds/downloads accumulate. Current Python inspection found a CPU PyTorch installation and no torchvision, so installed hardware does not establish a configured CUDA training environment.

Use this laptop for data audits, development, export verification and small student training after configuring a separate pinned environment. Use lazy loading, small measured batches with accumulation, compatible mixed precision when available, and conservative worker/cache counts. Avoid running large Android builds and RAM-heavy training together. Keep bulky training data outside the OneDrive-synced source directory; use an explicit data root, manifests and ignored local caches. Do not make the project depend on the old scripts' assumed A4000 GPU or local SDK paths.

Start with about **20–50k total stratified images** and **10–30k total speech clips** across multiple real sources/generator families, with deliberately separate development/calibration/test groups. Use a provisional 20–40GB data/cache cap, adjusting it from actual sample-size measurements; this is a bounded pilot, not a promise that every dataset fits that space. Acquire specific shards/records using published metadata where feasible. Streaming avoids eager RAM use but does not magically avoid reading an entire multi-terabyte corpus to find randomly scattered examples.

Scale a successful student experiment to **200–500k** well-chosen examples, then million-scale mixtures only if the learning curve and missing-generator/language results justify the additional effort. Full OpenFake/large multilingual/music corpora require external storage and more substantial GPU/CPU I/O resources. External SSD/storage and a dedicated/rented GPU server are potential next-stage resources; none was purchased, installed or started in this review. Training can use those resources while the final app remains entirely offline.

For product development, maintain a separate approved corpus/model list from the research experiment list. Start with datasets whose actual terms fit the intended use, licensed/consented real data and permitted generator examples. Noncommercial datasets and research-only pretrained weights may be useful comparisons when their conditions apply, but are not automatic ingredients for an app release. Resolve corpus/teacher terms **before** a costly run, because replacing restricted data or teacher supervision later may require retraining.

The near-term deliverable should be **one trustworthy image detector and one trustworthy speech detector**, with accurate status/coverage reporting, before adding more named forensic signals. The broadest practical offline system then comes from diverse well-labeled training, native-detail and semantic evidence, partial-media coverage, disciplined independent evaluation, and periodic tested model releases. The review identifies how to build that system; it does not manufacture an unverified universal detector or predict an accuracy gain without experiments.
